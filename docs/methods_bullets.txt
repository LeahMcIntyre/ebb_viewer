1. Recordings and preprocessing
• Datasets: Stxbp1 characterization, RNA KO and PHP.eB, each at pre, 3 weeks, 6 weeks and possibly 12 weeks; annotation files exist for every time point except 12 weeks
• Light cycle: lights on 6 AM (ZT 0), off 8 PM (ZT 14), same for all datasets
• TODO: species, strain, ages, genotypes, electrode type and placement, surgery, housing
• 4 channels: LFC, RSC, LSC, EMG. Raw EDF labels are EEG_1 to EEG_4, mapped to LFC, RSC, LSC, EMG by lab convention (position only)
• Recorded at 5000 Hz, units µV
• TODO: acquisition system, preamp, hardware high-pass cutoff
• Animal ID is the first _-delimited token of the EDF file name
• Restarted recordings: first 24 h of each chunk, joined in start-time order from the EDF header (2 chunks = 48 h, 3 = 72 h)
• Matching SWD and annotation files shifted by 24 h per chunk
• Chunks shorter than 24 h are not handled; a chunk longer than 24 h loses its extra time (a 28 h chunk loses 4 h)
• Gap between chunks is ignored, so clock time drifts by the restart offset
• Combined recordings: 4 animals from PHP.eB 6 weeks; their chunks started at very similar times of day
• Trimmed to the largest of 48 h or 72 h that fits; stop sample rounded up to a whole second; recordings under 48 h rejected
• Downsampled 5000 Hz to 250 Hz (factor 20), openseize Kaiser low-pass anti-alias filter: 125 Hz cutoff, 112.5 Hz passband edge, 137.5 Hz stopband edge, at most 0.1 dB passband loss, at least 40 dB stopband attenuation
• Max analyzable frequency 125 Hz
• Output: one 250 Hz EDF per animal with 1 s records
• Report: n animals at 48 h vs 72 h, n combined from restarts

2. Sleep staging (SPINDLE)
• SPINDLE via SleepyRat scores 4 s epochs as wake, NREM or REM, with a noise probability per epoch
• Scored with LFC + RSC + EMG for every animal
• Upload: the 250 Hz preprocessed EDF is cut to a channel subset (the two EEG channels plus EMG) with no other signal processing, and uploaded to SleepyRat; files already on the server are skipped and the label CSVs are downloaded
• Output: one CSV per animal with the state for each 4 s epoch, the noise probability and the class probabilities
• Noise cutoff: epochs with noise probability at or above 0.5 are artifacts (SleepyRat default; a file's own threshold is used when given)
• The untouched SPINDLE CSVs are kept in an _original folder
• Validation vs hand scoring: 40 files, 8 datasets, 2 raters, 49,025 epochs; the datasets include the Stxbp1 characterization, RNA KO and PHP.eB (pre and 6 weeks) sets used here
• Raw agreement 89.4%, after reclassification and gate 90.8%
• Citations: Miladinović et al. 2019, PLoS Comput Biol (SPINDLE); sleepyRat Team (2026), sleepyRat: Web platform for automated rodent sleep scoring based on EEG/EMG analysis [Software], https://sleepyrat.ai/. Scored June/July 2026

3. Reclassification
• Why: wake artifacts scored as NREM chop bouts and inflate bout counts
• Only NREM to wake changes are made
Per file, in 4 s epochs, starting from the original SPINDLE states:
1. Artifact epochs: noise probability at or above the file threshold (default 0.5)
2. Gap fill, radius 1: an NREM epoch becomes wake if it is an artifact epoch, or if it is clean but has an artifact epoch (any state) within 1 epoch on both sides; neighbors past the ends of the file count as clean
3. Gate decision: count the file's NREM bouts in the light period before and after gap fill; the light period is set by clock time from the EDF start time (06:00-19:59 light, 20:00-05:59 dark), and a bout belongs to the period its first epoch is in; if the count rises by more than 10%, the gate is applied
4. EMG gate (only when step 3 says so): EMG is channel 4 of the preprocessed 250 Hz EDF, with RMS computed per 4 s epoch; a flip is kept only if its epoch RMS is above median + 5 x MAD of the animal's EMG RMS over every epoch SPINDLE called NREM (artifact epochs included); MAD is the plain, unscaled median absolute deviation; the gate only cancels flips and never creates new ones
5. Output: reclass_state (gated state where the gate applied, otherwise the gap-filled state) and final_state, which all analysis reads; the original SPINDLE columns are left untouched; the manifest records for each animal whether the gate was applied
• Light period used for the gate decision because it best predicted where reclassification was reclassifying too much: bouts are not expected to become more fragmented in general, and especially not in the light period, when the movement artifacts being corrected are not really happening (Spearman rho 0.83 for light hours vs 0.58 for dark hours, on the annotated animals)
• Thresholds chosen by exploration, not formal optimization
• Effect on validation: gap fill +641 correct epochs, gate +68; NREM precision 90.1% to 94.1%
• Report: n animals gated

4. SWD handling
• Status: SWD review has not been done yet; it will be done for all datasets
• SWD times come from the lab MATLAB detector (TODO: citation)
• Why: SPINDLE can score SWDs in wake or REM as NREM, which could look like a genotype difference in fragmentation
• SWD files: one .mat file per animal with start and stop times in seconds; animals with no SWDs have an empty file
• An epoch counts as an SWD epoch if any part of it overlaps an interval (touching an edge exactly does not count)
• Review is a project-level setting (opt-in)
• Review window: the trace is shown with SWD intervals shaded and the state bar; the reviewer sets each SWD epoch to wake, NREM or REM; epochs on either side of an SWD can be edited too
• Every epoch overlapping an SWD is reviewed and corrected
• Rule: a wrong SWD epoch becomes the state the animal was in before and after the SWD (TODO: what to do when the states before and after differ)
• Output: swd_reclassification column (blank for unreviewed epochs) and final_state, where reviewed values take precedence over reclass_state; re-running reclassification keeps real edits
• Masking: the PSD step separately masks the SWD intervals, with no padding

5. QC and exclusion
• Six screening metrics are computed per animal from final_state: short NREM bouts per hour (1-2 epoch bouts, above 6), NREM bouts per dark hour (above 15), REM % of the file (above 10), NREM % of the file (below 30), wake % of the file (above 70), and the maximum REM % in any 2 h bin (at least 35)
• The two bout metrics are rates per hour, so recordings of different lengths are comparable; dark is 20:00-06:00
• The wake, NREM and REM % cutoffs were chosen from what was reasonable
• Cutoffs are screening aids, not validated; animals past any cutoff are flagged for review
• Overview: one boxplot per metric with every animal's raw point, a dashed line at each cutoff, and animals past a cutoff highlighted; the reviewer can flag animals from here
• Automatic flagging: when the metrics load, any animal with no status that is past a cutoff is set to needs review, with the metrics recorded as the reason; animals already marked ok, needs review or excluded are never changed; nothing is flagged before reclassification has been run
• The page can show the original SPINDLE states for comparison; the metrics follow whichever view is shown
• Review: a flagged animal is opened in the EEG/EMG viewer and marked OK, needs review or excluded; the reviewer is blind to genotype
• Exclude by judgment (very noisy EEG/EMG or heavy misclassification); every exclusion needs a written reason, saved in the animal's notes with the stage (Sleep QC or PSD review)
• An excluded animal is still computed but is not in PSD review, is marked in the sealed outputs, and is left out of the analysis notebooks by default
• An animal can also be excluded during PSD review, with a reason
• Report: n excluded, stage, reasons

6. PSD estimation
• Inputs: 250 Hz EDF, final_state, SPINDLE noise probabilities, optional SWD, annotation and drink masks
• Channels: the three EEG channels (LFC, RSC, LSC); EMG is not used
• Groups: wake, NREM, REM; one PSD per animal, channel and state
Per animal, in this order:
1. Compute each mask independently of the others (every mask is a per-sample True = keep array at 250 Hz):
   a. State masks: each 4 s epoch label of final_state is repeated 1,000 times (4 s x 250 Hz) to give a per-sample mask for wake, NREM and REM
   b. Commutator mask, on the unfiltered signal: |x| > 2000 µV; flagged samples closer than 1 s are merged; events longer than 10 samples are extended by 1 s after their end
   c. SPINDLE noise mask: epochs with noise probability at or above 0.5 are dropped
   d. SD mask, on the unfiltered signal: 180 s windows; each channel is z-scored; samples with |z| > 5 are flagged; flags within 0.5 s are merged; commutator-masked samples are left out of the mean and SD (the only place one mask uses another)
   e. SWD mask: SWD intervals masked, no padding
   f. Annotation mask: any Pinnacle annotation whose label contains "jerk" or "jump" masks the 2 s that follow it (jerks and jumps)
   g. Drink mask, only for animals rerun with it (section 7)
2. 60 Hz notch, applied only to the values that enter the PSD, after the commutator and SD masks have been computed (so those see unfiltered amplitudes): 8th-order Butterworth bandstop 57-63 Hz, zero-phase (forward-backward)
3. Intersect: the artifact masks (commutator, SPINDLE noise, SWD, annotation, drink) are ANDed together, and the result is intersected with each state mask; the SD mask is added only for the wake portion (for a group that mixes wake with sleep states, NREM and REM samples pass untouched); a sample flagged on any channel is masked on all channels
4. Welch PSD (openseize) on the kept samples: 0.5 Hz resolution (2 s segments), Hann window, 50% overlap, constant detrend, density scaling; units µV²/Hz; masked samples are removed (not zeroed) and the remainder is streamed to Welch, so a segment can span a removed gap
5. Stored for each result: frequencies, PSD, number of averaged segments, and the fraction of that state's samples removed by each mask
• SD mask applied to wake only because applied to NREM it masked out too much, including real features such as sleep spindles
• Groups with no surviving samples (for example an animal with no REM) are skipped
• Notch order matters little: late vs early notch differed by at most 1.5% outside the notch (Stxbp1 characterization, 24 animals)
• Report: fraction of samples removed by each mask

7. PSD review and drink mask
• Review loop: each included animal's PSDs are reviewed in the PSD visualizer and marked ok or needs drink mask
• Artifact criterion: drinking shows up as sidebands in the spectrum and is recognized by eye; no automatic detector was tried
• Drink events: video from the PVFS files, downsampled to 1 frame per second, is classified as drinking or not drinking by a network developed in the lab (unpublished); the output is a 1 Hz mask (True = drinking) saved as a .npy file
• Video aligned to the EEG by a manually annotated offset between EEG start and video start (no other clock correction)
• Offsets file: a CSV with an animal ID and the offset in seconds (positive means the video started after the EEG)
• Drink mask: each event plus or minus 2 s, upsampled to 250 Hz, shifted by the offset
• Flagged animals are recomputed with the same parameters plus the drink mask, then verified: Mask worked marks the animal ok, Redo mask sends it back for another try
• Reruns go to separate files and never overwrite the original PSDs; Finalize uses the drink-mask version where there is one
• The visualizer can show the inverse filter and the line-noise interpolation, but both are display-only; the stored PSDs do not include them
• Finalize waits until no animal is unreviewed, flagged or waiting for verification
• Animals can also be excluded here, with a reason
• Report: n animals needing drink mask

8. PSD analysis
• Input: the sealed psds_final.pkl, one PSD per animal, state and channel; excluded animals are left out by default (a setting includes them)
• Low-frequency correction: the 0.5-3.5 Hz bins (seven bins) are multiplied by factors that undo the hardware roll-off, from 2.49 at the lowest bin down to 1.01; applied in the analysis, not stored in the PSD files (coefficients computed by Claudia; TODO: get details of how)
• Line noise interpolated: 56-64 Hz covers the 60 Hz peak and the gap left by the notch; 118-122 Hz covers the 120 Hz peak, which the notch does not reach (a run without the notch would use 58-62 Hz)
• Groups: animals are assigned to groups from a typed dictionary or a CSV; spectra are plotted per group and per time point
• Spectra are shown from 0.5 to 110 Hz
• Normalized spectrum = interpolated spectrum / its own power from 1-100 Hz
• Band power is the area under the spectrum (trapezoid rule), for raw and normalized spectra: 1-4 Hz (delta), 4-8 Hz (theta), 8-12 Hz (alpha), 12-30 Hz (beta), 30-70 Hz (low gamma), 70-100 Hz (high gamma)

9. Sleep by hour
• Input: final_state, one state per 4 s epoch (each repeated to one state per second); excluded animals are left out by default; the original SPINDLE states can be used instead for comparison
• Start time is read from the EDF header; a recording that ends before it reaches 6 AM is skipped
• Recording rotated so the first 6 AM (lights on, ZT 0) is position 0, then cut into 2 h bins; ZT = (clock hour - 6) mod 24
• Time zone America/Chicago: the real elapsed time to the first 6 AM is used (compared in UTC), so a clock change inside a recording is handled
• Per state (wake, NREM, REM) and bin: fraction of time, bout count, mean bout duration
• Bout: an unbroken run of epochs in the same state, with no minimum length
• Bouts are counted within each bin on its own: a bout that crosses a bin edge is split and counted once in each bin, with only its in-bin length
• Rotation: the pre-6 AM part of the recording is appended to the end, so a bout of the same state on both sides of that join is counted as one
• If the rotated recording does not divide into whole bins, the last bin is shorter than 2 h (its time fraction uses its actual length)
• Folding: bins are folded onto a 24 h day and averaged across days within each animal, so each animal has one value per ZT bin (the number of days averaged is recorded; a mean bout duration averages only the days that had a bout)
• Whole-file values: one number per animal and state over the whole recording (no rotation): fraction of time, bout count, mean bout duration and bouts per hour
• Group plots: group mean ± SEM at each ZT bin, with the dark period (8 PM-6 AM, ZT 14-24) shaded

10. Software
• ebb is a refactor of EBB (Caudill, https://github.com/mscaudill/ebb); it keeps the backbone of the thresholding, PSD computation and use of openseize
• Added: updated thresholding, many more masking options, SPINDLE integration, reclassification, SWD review, QC and a GUI
• Structure: ebb is a processing library with no GUI; ebb_viewer is the Qt 6 application that drives it; the processing also runs from scripts and notebooks
• GUI: PyQt6 with pyqtgraph for the trace viewer
• Project state: manifest.json records each animal's status (Sleep QC, PSD review, exclusion and notes, SWD progress, whether the EMG gate was applied) and the project's channel combo; it is written atomically, statuses change only through manifest methods, and every exclusion needs a reason
• Reproducibility: the settings of each PSD run are saved in psd_params.json
• No run log yet: trim choices and SPINDLE version are not recorded
• Analysis notebooks (in analysis/): PSD analysis and sleep by hour (a quick-run notebook and a walkthrough notebook)
• Versions (ebb-dev environment): Python 3.13.14, numpy 2.4.6, scipy 1.18.0, pandas 3.0.5, openseize 1.3.0, matplotlib 3.11.1, PyQt6 6.11.0, pyqtgraph 0.14.0 (TODO: confirm the datasets were processed with these)
• Code: https://github.com/LeahMcIntyre/ebb_viewer
